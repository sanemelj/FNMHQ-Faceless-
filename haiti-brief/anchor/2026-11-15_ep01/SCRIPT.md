**A02 — How it started (~30 s)**
On October eighteenth, gangs ambushed an armored vehicle near Port-au-Prince. Inside were four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force. They have been missing ever since.
Nayib Bukele [Nah-yeeb Boo-keh-leh] answered on X: "We have already finished with the gangs in El Salvador, and we can finish with you too."
[B-ROLL: helicopters taking off; quote card over footage · sources: AP via Al Jazeera; Bukele on X]

**A03 — The build-up (~30 s)**
More than two thousand Salvadoran soldiers came next, with armored vehicles and helicopters. Most of them are not part of the UN-backed force. They are here under a separate deal between the two governments.
Washington raised concerns, and asked for operations to be coordinated.
[B-ROLL: armored vehicle street, troops · sources: AFP; Al Jazeera; Axios via The Haitian Times]

**A04 — The house (~30 s)**
On November tenth, Bukele posted video of a helicopter operation. He says his forces destroyed the house of Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò San Jou [Lahn-moh Sahn Zhoo], and the headquarters of the 400 Mawozo [four hundred Ma-woe-zo] gang. Haiti's prime minister thanked him publicly.
[B-ROLL: helicopter strike footage, smoke over compound · source: Bukele on X; The Haitian Times]

**A05 — The stadium (~35 s)**
Two days later, Haitian police and Salvadoran forces took back Sylvio Cator [Sil-vee-oh Ka-tor], the national stadium. Gangs had held it since 2024.
Bukele's video shows armed robot dogs on the pitch. He says his forces found human remains inside, and that the stadium is now a base, to push into gang territory.
[B-ROLL: robot dogs, stands, aerial · NO images of remains · sources: Haitian National Police via AFP; Bukele; HaitiLibre]

**A06 — Friday night (~25 s, ON CAMERA: no footage)**
On Friday night, strong explosions were heard across central Port-au-Prince. Reports say police used explosive drones against gangs in Martissant [Mar-tee-sahn]. There is no official toll yet.
[B-ROLL: none (on camera) + map pin Martissant on the wall · sources: Journal l'Observatoire; Haiti Brief source on the ground]

**A07 — The cost (~30 s)**
Many Haitians are cheering. The numbers explain why: the UN counted at least one thousand one hundred sixty-seven people killed between July and September.
But the same report says more than half of those killed or injured were hit during security operations against gangs.
[B-ROLL: city aerials, no people identifiable · source: BINUH via UN News, Nov. 12]

**A08 — What's next (~30 s)**
On Sunday, Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé [Feess Ay-may] is due in El Salvador to talk about the future of these troops.
What we still don't know: the terms of the deal, who commands these soldiers, and what happened to the four missing technicians.
[B-ROLL: map Port-au-Prince → San Salvador · source: AP]
