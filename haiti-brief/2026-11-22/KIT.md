```
Before announcing a new prison deal with El Salvador, Haiti's government had already signed a contract to build three prisons. The watchdog group FJKL revealed it in March: about $85 million, with a 50-year concession. The Haitian Times reported that the state would have to guarantee the prisons are at least 75% full. FJKL estimates total payments could top $6 billion over 50 years (its own estimate).

Justice Minister Patrick Pélissier now says a Salvadoran company will build three new prisons with a Haitian partner. He has not said whether this replaces the earlier contract, or what the new prisons will cost.
```

**Deskripsyon:**
```
About a month ago, El Salvador sent more than 2,000 troops to Haiti after four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force went missing in an ambush in Croix-des-Bouquets on Oct. 18. Here is what has changed since, and what it has cost.

The strikes: Bukele's videos of helicopter strikes on gang leaders' homes, the retaking of the Sylvio Cator stadium on Nov. 12, the EDH headquarters and the Portail Léogane police station, and the Nov. 18 "Vertières" strike on the home of gang leader Jeff Larose, known as Gwo Lwa, in Canaan. It is still not known whether he was there.

The politics: marches for Bukele in Port-au-Prince, Jacmel and Cap-Haïtien; a security agreement approved on Nov. 17 whose text has not been published; no end date for the troops; U.S. reservations; Bukele's bid for a third term; and a plan for three new prisons with a Salvadoran company.

The cost: three Salvadoran soldiers dead after an explosion at a camp, the four technicians still missing, and calls from Amnesty International to publish the agreement and the rules on the use of force. The UN says 4,595 people were killed by armed violence in Haiti from January to October 2026.
```
